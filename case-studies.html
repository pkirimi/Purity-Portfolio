HerCycle

How I built and shipped an AI powered women's health application.

A solo product engineering case study covering the problem, product decisions, AI integration, technical architecture, challenges and what I learned from taking HerCycle from an idea to a working mobile product.

ROLE AI Engineer & Product Builder
PRODUCT Mobile AI Application
PLATFORM Android
STATUS Live product
AT A GLANCE

The idea

Build a women's health product that adapts to different reproductive health journeys instead of treating cycle tracking as the entire experience.

My role

I worked across product thinking, interface design, application development, AI integration, backend services, testing and deployment.

The result

A working mobile application combining multiple health journeys, personalised experiences, tracking features and AI capabilities.

01

THE PROBLEM

Women's health is not one journey.

Women's reproductive health changes across different stages of life. Someone may be tracking their cycle today, trying to conceive later, then move into pregnancy or postpartum care.

I wanted to build a product that could adapt to those changing needs rather than creating a single experience focused only on period tracking.

That shaped the product around multiple journeys while keeping the everyday experience simple enough to use regularly.

02

THE PRODUCT

More than a period tracker.

HerCycle was designed around six broad journeys: cycle tracking, trying to conceive, pregnancy, postpartum, perimenopause and exploring.

01

Personalised AI assistance

AI capabilities are integrated into the product experience to provide personalised support based on the user's context and journey.

02

Cycle tracking

Users can set up their cycle and track information used to support personalised predictions and insights.

03

Partner mode

A dedicated experience allows partners to receive appropriate updates and provide support without exposing unnecessary personal details.

04

Journey specific content

The product extends beyond cycle tracking into areas such as fertility, pregnancy and postpartum experiences.

03

ENGINEERING

Building the system behind it.

I approached HerCycle as a real software product, not simply as an AI demonstration. That meant thinking about the application, data, authentication, backend services, AI capabilities and deployment together.

FRONTEND Expo / React Native
BACKEND Supabase
AI AI APIs & personalised assistance
DEVELOPMENT Python · GitHub · Expo tooling
DISTRIBUTION EAS Build · Google Play
04

PRODUCT DECISIONS

What I chose and why.

01

Build a product, not just an AI demo.

AI is only one part of a useful application. Users also need onboarding, navigation, data handling, authentication and an interface that makes the underlying capability useful.

02

Organise the experience around user journeys.

The product was structured around different reproductive health stages rather than forcing every user through the same experience.

03

Keep health information responsible.

The product is designed to support tracking and access to information. It should not position an AI system as a replacement for professional medical care.

05

CHALLENGES

Shipping was harder than building.

The difficult part was not only writing application code. I also had to work through the infrastructure and operational problems required to turn the code into a usable product.

Authentication Debugging account and authentication behaviour.
Mobile builds Working through Expo and EAS build configuration.
App distribution Preparing the application for Google Play.
Privacy requirements Working through store requirements around privacy documentation and data handling.
Product configuration Connecting application services and getting the different pieces working together.
06

OUTCOME

From idea to shipped product.

HerCycle became a working mobile application rather than a concept limited to a design or prototype.

The product brought together multiple user journeys, personalised experiences, data handling, AI capabilities and mobile deployment into one system.

The most valuable outcome for me was the experience of taking ownership across the entire product lifecycle: identifying a problem, designing the experience, engineering the system, debugging it and shipping it.
